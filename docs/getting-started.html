No setup required. This page explains the idea. Check the release status before looking for an installer.

No technical background needed. Start with one question: when AI helps with your work, what should still make sense tomorrow?

1. Decide what must not be lost

Write down the things people will need later: goals, decisions, rules, who owns the work, what is happening now, and how you know it is right.

Keep the list short. Clear facts are more useful than a long chat that only one AI tool understands.

2. Make ownership clear

Separate the reusable application, your personal data, and files that can be rebuilt. Then an update can change the application without overwriting your work.

example-application/ ├── application/ # reusable workflow ├── my-state/ # goals, progress, and decisions ├── history/ # records of change └── generated/ # views that can be rebuilt

The names may differ. The important part is knowing what belongs to you.

3. Ask for access; do not hand out unlimited access

A tool can say what it needs to do. People and the project decide what it may actually do. “The workflow asked for it” should never be the whole permission check.

4. Keep the steps separate

Planning, approving, changing, and checking are different steps. Keeping them separate makes it easier to see what happened.

5. Do not tie the work to one tool

The application should remain understandable if a model, AI tool, or computer changes. A new tool should say what it can and cannot do.

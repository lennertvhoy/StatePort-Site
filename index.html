A lasting home for AI-assisted work

StatePortApplications that keep their state.

Most AI work happens in a chat window — easy to start, easy to lose, and hard to check afterwards. StatePort runs durable, user-owned AI applications on your own computer: the installed application is the product, and its goals, decisions, and history live in files you can open, read, and take with you.

  • Applications that remember. StudyState, the first StatePort application, is a study coach whose learning state survives sessions and restarts — conversation belongs to the application, not the other way around.
  • Change you can review and undo. A reflection becomes an exact proposed change you explicitly apply; every applied change keeps a persistent Undo and linked receipts.
  • Deployments you approve first. StatePort inspects a project, proposes an exact container plan, and runs it on local rootless Podman with health checks — only after you approve the plan, with receipts at every step.

The idea

The application is the product.

StatePort runs durable, user-owned AI applications. The installed application is what you see and use; the conversation belongs to that application; advanced controls appear when you ask for them, not before. Underneath, an agent may do the work — but it never owns the truth: important change is proposed, approved, applied, and recorded, so it stays reviewable and undoable.

Chat and agent sessions help move things forward. They do not become the only place your work exists.

The product today

One platform, two pillars.

Both pillars run locally in the published alpha candidate, and both follow the same rule: nothing consequential happens without an explicit approval and a receipt.

Pillar one — applications

StudyState, a study coach that remembers

The first StatePort application shows what to do next and why, lets you start, pause, and redirect, turns reflection into a change you review and apply, and keeps a persistent Undo with receipts. Its state survives restarts.

Pillar two — deployments

Container deployments you approve first

Why it matters

Three things should remain true.

01

Your state stays yours

Each application is a template of durable state files and scripts you can read, edit, and carry with you — not a session locked inside a chat.

02

Important change stays explicit

A proposal, permission decision, applied result, validation, and your final sign-off stay separate facts.

03

Providers are profiled

StatePort runs Codex CLI in the current alpha; Pi is a reference direction and OpenCode is unqualified. Capabilities and behavior differ between providers and are spelled out explicitly.

How it works

From definition to evidence.

01 / WRAP

Wrap a coding agent

StatePort runs agents like Codex headlessly in an environment you control — your machine, or an isolated container.

02 / TEMPLATE

Give the work a home

Each application is a template of durable state files plus the scripts the agent is allowed to run.

03 / RUN

Let it run, governed

StatePort plans the work, runs the agent, asks approval when it matters, and records what changed. You can watch, pause, redirect, or roll back; the agent never owns the truth.

04 / KEEP

Keep the result

Decisions, files, and history stay in your instance. Change the provider or model and the work is still there.
